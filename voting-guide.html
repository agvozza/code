Ballot proposals

Rule of thumb: YES = make the change, NO = keep things as they are. Ignore the title and ask what changes if it passes.
Likely youNO
  • Plain English: should Michigan hold a convention in 2027 to rewrite the whole state constitution?
  • YES = voters elect delegates, and they can rewrite anything (abortion rights, the independent redistricting commission, voting rules). Voters get a final vote on the result.
  • NO = the constitution stays as is. It can still be changed one amendment at a time.
  • Why NO fits you: whoever turns out in the delegate election controls the rewrite, and the protections you probably care about would be on the table.
Likely youYES
  • Plain English: should utilities and big state contractors be banned from political donations?
  • YES = DTE, Consumers Energy and companies with over $250,000 in state contracts (plus their officers, major owners, board members and immediate families) can't give to politicians. Groups running issue ads must disclose donors.
  • NO = current rules stay.
  • Fine print: it doesn't ban dark-money nonprofits, out-of-state donors who aren't state contractors, or union PACs. The campaign for it is funded by out-of-state groups ($5.4M from All Hands on Deck, $1.4M from the Sixteen Thirty Fund). Opponents include Consumers Energy and the Michigan Chamber.
  • Why YES fits you: it's a real limit on utility and contractor money, and the Democratic Party supports it. It is a partial fix, not a cure-all.
Likely youYES (nonpartisan, your call)
  • Plain English: renew the city property tax that pays for police, fire, and road and sidewalk upkeep.
  • YES = the tax continues at 20 mills, the rate voters originally approved. State rollback rules had lowered it, so your bill could rise slightly versus today.
  • NO = the funding expires or shrinks, which can mean service cuts.
Low stakesLean YES (your call)
  • Plain English: create a city board to study healthcare costs and access and recommend a plan.
  • YES = the board exists. It can only recommend. The city can't carry out its plan, and the council must publish it.
  • NO = no board.
  • Catch: it was pushed by Community Action Michigan, a committee that raised about $1.4M and has partly opaque funding. The same effort is in five other cities. It does little either way, so vote based on whether you want the study.
