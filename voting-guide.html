Proposals

Rule of thumb: YES = make the change, NO = keep things as they are. Ignore the title and read what changes if it passes.
Likely youNO
  • The ballot asks: shall a convention of elected delegates be convened in 2027 to draft a general revision of the State Constitution?
  • YES = delegates are elected and can rewrite any part of the constitution, including abortion rights, the redistricting commission and voting rules. Voters get a final vote on the result.
  • NO = the constitution stays as is. It can still be amended one change at a time.
  • Why NO fits you: whoever turns out for the delegate election controls the rewrite, and the protections you probably care about would be open. The Democratic Party opposes it.
Likely youYES
  • Despite the long title, the ballot lists three changes if it passes:
  • 1. Regulated electric and gas utilities, contractors with over $250,000 a year in government contracts, and people and organizations with substantial connections to them could not make direct or indirect campaign contributions to those who run for or hold offices that affect them.
  • 2. Disclosure rules for spending on political communications would also cover communications that clearly identify candidates or ballot questions, even if they do not say to vote for or against.
  • 3. The rule requiring disclosure of who paid for political communications would expressly cover internet communications.
  • YES = those three changes happen. NO = current rules stay.
  • Limits: it does not ban dark-money nonprofits or out-of-state donors who aren't state contractors, and union PACs are mostly untouched. The campaign for it has been funded by out-of-state groups. Opponents include Consumers Energy and the Michigan Chamber of Commerce.
  • Why YES fits you: it is a real limit on utility and contractor money, and the Democratic Party supports it. It is a partial fix, not a cure-all.
Likely you (nonpartisan)YES
  • Important: this is not just a renewal. State rollback rules cut the city tax from 20 mills to 19.2214. A YES restores it to 20 mills, an increase of 0.7786 mills.
  • In dollars: about $0.78 more per $1,000 of taxable value. A home with $60,000 taxable value would pay roughly $47 more a year. Taxable value is usually lower than market value.
  • It funds police, fire, and road and sidewalk maintenance for five years (Jan 1, 2027 to Dec 31, 2031) and would raise about $2,585,101 in its first year.
  • NO = the rate stays at 19.2214, the city collects less, and services may be trimmed.
Low stakesLean YES
  • The ballot asks whether the city charter should require a Citizens' Advisory Healthcare Action Board to create a Lansing Healthcare Action Plan, recommend ways to address healthcare costs and access, set the board's powers and makeup, and require publication, a public overview and implementation recommendations.
  • YES = the board becomes part of the charter. City Council said in public that the city cannot carry out the board's plan itself, though that sentence is not in the ballot text.
  • NO = no board.
  • Backed by Community Action Michigan, a committee that reported about $1.4 million raised with partly undisclosed funding. It does little either way, so vote on whether you want the study.
