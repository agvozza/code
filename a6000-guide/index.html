    Level 1 · Intelligent Auto

    Point, half-press, shoot. The camera makes every decision. Stay here as long as you like.

    Level 1Intelligent AutoPoint and shootLevel 2Scene SelectionTell it what you're shootingLevel 3A modeControl the look yourself

    Quick start

    1. Turn it onFlip the ON / OFF switch around the shutter button (13). The lens extends by itself, so let it.
    2. Set the mode dial to iAutoThat's the camera icon with a little “i” on the top dial (15). The camera handles everything for you.
    3. ZoomSlide the W — T lever on the side of the lens (20). W = wide, T = zoomed in (telephoto).
    4. Half-press to focusPress the shutter button (12) halfway and hold it. Green boxes and a beep mean it's in focus.
    5. Press all the way to take the photoKeep holding and press the rest of the way down. For video, press the red MOVIE button (6) to start and again to stop.
    Screen went black? There's an eye sensor above the screen. When your hand or body is close to the viewfinder (1), the camera turns off the back screen and uses the viewfinder instead. Move your hand away and the screen comes back.

    Getting the most out of Auto

    Let it read the scene

    After you frame a shot, give it a moment. A small icon at the top of the screen shows what the camera thinks it's looking at (a person, a landscape, a night scene, a close-up) and it sets itself up for that.

    Choose what's sharp

    The camera focuses on whatever looks most important. If it picks the wrong thing, put your subject in the middle, half-press and keep holding, then move the camera to frame the shot and press all the way.

    Faces come first

    It draws a box around each face it finds and focuses on them. In a group shot, check there's a box on someone before you shoot.

    Get close for food and flowers

    Zoom all the way out (W) and move in. The lens can focus as close as about 25 cm. If it won't focus, lean back a little.

    Indoors and at night

    Hold still and tuck your elbows in, or lean on a wall. The flash only fires if you pop it up with ⚡ (3). Many churches and museums don't allow it.

    Video any time

    Press the red MOVIE button (6) to start and stop. Hold the camera steady and move slowly.

    Like iAuto, but in tricky light it may take several shots and combine them. A bit slower.

    Automatic exposure, but you can change settings like ISO and white balance.

    You choose the shutter speed. Fast speeds freeze motion; slow speeds blur it.

    Loads saved settings. If you land here by accident, go back to iAuto.

    🎞Movie

    Video with extra controls. You don't need it, because the red MOVIE button records in any mode.

    Lost in the settings? Turn the dial back to iAuto. It ignores most of the custom settings and just works.

    Level 2 · Scene Selection

    Still automatic, but you tell the camera what you're shooting, and it uses settings made for that situation.

    Level 1Intelligent AutoPoint and shootLevel 2Scene SelectionTell it what you're shootingLevel 3A modeControl the look yourself

    How to choose a scene

    1. Turn the mode dial to SCNOn the top dial (15). The camera may show the list of scenes straight away.
    2. If it doesn't, open the listPress MENU (4) → camera tab → Scene Selection.
    3. Pick one and press ●Move with ▲▼ or by turning the control wheel (8). The scene's symbol shows on the screen while you shoot.
    4. Shoot as usualHalf-press to focus, press fully to take the photo. Remember to change the scene when the situation changes.

    The scenes, and when to use them

    Portrait

    Use it for
    People at viewpoints, friends at a café table, travel photos of you taken by someone else.
    What it does
    Softens the background and gives skin a gentle, natural look.
    Tips
    Zoom in a little (toward T) and step back. Leave plenty of distance between the person and the background. Open shade, or the sun behind you, gives the most flattering faces.

    Sports Action

    Use it for
    Trams, cyclists, street performers, gondolas, kids running, anything moving.
    What it does
    Uses a very fast shutter to freeze motion and keeps refocusing while you half-press. Holding the shutter down takes a burst of photos.
    Tips
    Start following the subject before the moment happens and hold the shutter through it. Keep the best one and delete the rest later.

    Macro

    Use it for
    Pastries, market stalls, flowers, details on old doors and tiles.
    What it does
    Sets the camera up for focusing close.
    Tips
    Zoom all the way out (W). The lens focuses as close as about 25 cm, so if it won't focus, back up a little. Keep the flash down.

    Landscape

    Use it for
    Mountains, coastlines, lakes, the view from a tower or bell tower.
    What it does
    Keeps everything sharp from front to back and makes blues and greens vivid.
    Tips
    Use the grid lines (already on) to keep the horizon level. Put the horizon on the top or bottom grid line, not through the middle.

    Sunset

    Use it for
    Sunsets, sunrises and golden hour over the sea or rooftops.
    What it does
    Deepens the reds and oranges so the sky looks the way you remember it.
    Tips
    Don't stare at the sun through the viewfinder. Keep shooting for 10 to 20 minutes after the sun goes down; the sky often gets better.

    Night Scene

    Use it for
    City lights, lit-up bridges and cathedrals after dark, with no people close up.
    What it does
    Keeps the shutter open longer so the scene stays dark and atmospheric instead of flat.
    Tips
    The camera must be completely still. Rest it on a wall or railing and use the 2-second self-timer (◀ Drive Mode) so pressing the button doesn't shake it. People in the shot will be blurry.

    Hand-held Twilight

    Use it for
    Night streets and evening scenes when you can't rest the camera on anything.
    What it does
    Takes a quick burst of several shots and combines them into one sharper, cleaner photo.
    Tips
    Often the best night choice for travel. Hold still until it finishes clicking. Works best when nothing in the scene is moving fast.

    Night Portrait

    Use it for
    A person in front of city lights at night.
    What it does
    Fires the flash for the person and keeps the shutter open for the lights behind them.
    Tips
    Pop up the flash first with ⚡. Ask the person to stay still for a second after the flash, and keep the camera steady too.

    Anti Motion Blur

    Use it for
    Museums, churches, restaurants and other dim places where flash isn't allowed.
    What it does
    Raises the sensitivity and combines a quick burst of shots to cut down on blur.
    Tips
    Hold still until it finishes. Great for interiors, paintings and stained glass without a flash.

    Also on the dial: Sweep Panorama ▭

    Sweep Panorama

    Use it for
    Extra-wide views: a whole coastline, a mountain range, a piazza or a skyline.
    How
    Turn the mode dial to ▭. Press the shutter once (don't hold it), then turn your body smoothly in the direction of the arrow on screen until the bar fills up.
    Tips
    Turn at your waist and keep the camera level, at a steady, unhurried speed. If you go too fast or too slow the camera tells you, so just try again. People who are moving can come out cut in half.

    Level 3 · A mode (Aperture Priority)

    The best way to learn photography. You make one creative choice and the camera handles the maths. Turn the mode dial (15) to A.

    Level 1Intelligent AutoPoint and shootLevel 2Scene SelectionTell it what you're shootingLevel 3A modeControl the look yourself

    1 · The one setting you control: aperture (f-number)

    Turn the control dial on top (16) or the control wheel on the back (8). You'll see a number like F3.5 or F8 change on screen.

    Small number
    F3.5 – F5.6
    The background goes soft and blurry, so your subject pops. Good for portraits, food and flowers. It also lets in more light.
    Big number
    F8 – F11
    Everything is sharp from front to back. Good for landscapes, architecture and big group shots.
    Zoom changes it too. This lens goes down to F3.5 when zoomed out (W) but only F5.6 when zoomed in (T). For the blurriest background, zoom in, stand back, and get close to your subject with distance behind them.

    2 · Watch the shutter speed

    The camera picks the shutter speed for you and shows it as 1/250, 1/60, and so on. If it drops below about 1/50, photos may come out blurry from hand shake. To fix it, pick a smaller f-number or make sure ISO (▶) is set to AUTO.

    3 · Cheat sheet

    SituationApertureAlso try

    4 · Too bright or too dark?

    Exposure compensation brightens or darkens the next photos. On this camera it's in MENU → camera tab → Exposure Comp. Move it toward + to brighten or − to darken. Remember to set it back to 0 afterwards. Bright sky behind your subject? Try switching Metering (▼) to Spot.

    Good to know: photos taken in A mode are saved as RAW files. These are top quality for editing on a computer, but they can't be sent to your phone over Wi-Fi. If you want to share A-mode shots on the go, set Quality in the Fn menu to RAW & JPEG.

    What every button does

    Several buttons have been customized for this camera; those are marked in orange. Tap a dot or a row to highlight it.

    Standard Sony buttonCustomized for you

    Back of the camera

    1. 1
      Viewfinder
      Hold it up to your eye on bright sunny days when the screen is hard to see. The back screen switches off automatically.
    2. 2
      Diopter dial
      If the viewfinder looks blurry, turn this little wheel until the text inside looks sharp to your eye.
    3. 3
      Flash pop-up ⚡
      The flash never pops up on its own. Press this to raise it; push it back down with your finger when you're done. If the flash is down, it won't fire.
    4. 4
      MENU
      The full settings menu. Press it again to get out. You can usually leave this alone.
    5. 5
      AEL button → Eye AF custom
      For portraits, point at a person and hold this button. The camera finds and focuses on their eye (a small box appears on it). Keep holding, then press the shutter. When you're viewing photos it zooms in instead (🔍+).
    6. 6
      MOVIE (red dot)
      Press once to start recording video and again to stop. It works in any mode.
    7. 7
      Fn (Function)
      Your quick-settings screen with 12 shortcuts. See Fn menu. When you're viewing photos, this button sends the photo to your phone.
    8. 8
      Control wheel
      This wheel turns, and you can also press it at the top, bottom, left, right and centre. Each press does something different; see the close-up below.
    9. 9
      Playback ▶
      Look at the photos you've taken. Half-press the shutter to go back to shooting.
    10. 10
      C2 → Flash Comp. / Delete 🗑 custom
      While shooting, it makes the flash weaker or stronger (handy if faces look washed out). While viewing photos, it deletes the photo on screen.
    11. 11
      Tilting screen
      Pull the bottom of the screen out to tilt it up or down, so you can shoot from the hip or hold the camera over a crowd.

    Close-up: the control wheel

    1. ▲
      Top · DISP
      Changes what's shown on screen: all the info, a clean view, or a level/histogram. Keep pressing to cycle through them.
    2. ▶
      Right · ISO
      ISO is how sensitive the camera is to light. Leave it on AUTO unless you're experimenting. Higher numbers work in the dark but look grainier.
    3. ▼
      Bottom · Metering Mode custom
      Controls how the camera measures brightness. Multi (the default) is right 95% of the time. Use Spot when your subject is against a bright sky or window.
    4. ◀
      Left · Drive Mode
      Single shot, burst (holding the shutter takes many photos, good for moving things), or self-timer (good for group photos, with the camera propped on a wall).
    5. ●
      Centre · Focus Area custom
      Chooses where the camera focuses. Wide lets the camera pick. Center always focuses on the middle of the frame. Flexible Spot lets you move the box yourself. In menus, centre = OK/select.
    6. ↻
      Turning the wheel
      Scrolls through menus and photos. In A mode it also changes the aperture (see Level 3).

    Top of the camera

    1. 12
      Shutter button
      Half-press to focus, then press fully to take the photo. A half-press also gets you out of any menu or playback, back to shooting.
    2. 13
      ON / OFF switch
      The collar around the shutter button. Turn the camera off before you change lenses or open the battery door.
    3. 14
      C1 → Peaking Level custom
      Peaking outlines the sharpest parts of the picture in colour, so you can see what's in focus. This button switches it between High, Mid, Low and Off. If you see coloured fuzzy edges on screen, that's peaking. It doesn't appear in your photos.
    4. 15
      Mode dial
      Picks the shooting mode. iAuto is Level 1, SCN is Level 2 and A is Level 3. Every position is explained in the mode dial at a glance.
    5. 16
      Control dial
      In A mode, turn this to change the aperture (the f-number). In the Fn menu, turn it to change the selected setting.
    6. 17
      Hot shoe
      For an external flash or microphone. You won't need it on the trip.
    7. 18
      Built-in flash
      This is where the flash pops up from when you press ⚡ (3). Many museums and churches don't allow flash, so keep it down there.
    8. 19
      Lens ring (zoom / focus)
      Usually it zooms. Right after the camera focuses (while you're still half-pressing), turning it fine-tunes the focus by hand. That's the “DMF” setting.
    9. 20
      Power zoom lever (W — T)
      The easiest way to zoom: slide it toward T to zoom in and toward W to zoom out.

    Side of the camera

    1. 19
      Lens ring
      Zoom / manual focus touch-up (see above).
    2. 20
      Zoom lever
      W = wide, T = tight.
    3. 21
      Charging port (behind the flap)
      Open the flap marked MULTI. The micro-USB port charges the battery inside the camera. The other port is HDMI, for a TV.
    4. 22
      Wi-Fi
      Built in, for sending photos to your phone (see Viewing & phone).

    The Fn menu: your 12 shortcuts

    Press Fn, move around with the wheel buttons (▲▼◀▶), and turn the control dial (top) to change the highlighted setting. Press ● to see all the options. Half-press the shutter to leave. Tap a tile to jump to its explanation below. In iAuto, some tiles are greyed out because the camera is choosing for you.

    Function menu
    ◯ / dial: Change · ● More · MENU ↩
    1. 1
      SteadyShot
      Set to: On · Function Upper1
      Image stabilisation, which cancels out shaky hands. Leave it ON (switch it off only if the camera is on a tripod).
    2. 2
      Smile/Face Detect.
      Set to: Face Detection On · Function Upper2
      Finds faces and focuses on them automatically. A box appears around each face it sees.
    3. 3
      Metering Mode
      Set to: Multi · Function Upper3
      How the camera measures brightness. Multi looks at the whole scene and is right most of the time. Spot measures only the centre, which is useful for a person against a bright window or sky. Also on the ▼ button.
    4. 4
      Grid Line
      Set to: Grid on · Function Upper4
      Draws lines over the screen (not on the photo). Use them to keep horizons and buildings straight, and to place your subject on a line instead of dead centre (the rule of thirds).
    5. 5
      Peaking Level
      Set to: High · Function Upper5
      Highlights in-focus edges with colour so you can see what's sharp. It doesn't affect the photo. Also on C1. Set to Off if it's distracting.
    6. 6
      Quality
      Set to: RAW · Function Upper6
      File type. RAW = maximum quality for editing on a computer, but the files are large and can't go to your phone. RAW & JPEG saves both, which is the best choice if you want to share from the road. Fine = JPEG only, small and easy.
    7. 7
      Focus Mode
      Set to: DMF (Direct Manual Focus) · Function Lower1
      The camera autofocuses when you half-press. While you're still holding, you can turn the lens ring to fine-tune focus by hand. For moving subjects, switch to AF-C (continuous) so it keeps tracking them.
    8. 8
      Shoot Mode
      Set to: Follows the mode dial · Function Lower2
      Shows the current mode. Change modes with the mode dial on top instead.
    9. 9
      White Balance
      Set to: Auto · Function Lower3
      Keeps colours natural under different light (sun, shade, indoor bulbs). Leave it on AWB. If photos look too orange or too blue, check it's still on AWB.
    10. 10
      DRO/Auto HDR
      Set to: DRO Level 4 · Function Lower4
      Brightens dark shadows in high-contrast scenes, like a bright sky with a shady street. Lv4 is a fairly strong setting. If photos look flat or grey, try Auto or a lower level.
    11. 11
      Zebra
      Set to: Off · Function Lower5
      Draws striped warnings on areas that are too bright. Mostly used for video. Fine to leave off.
    12. 12
      Flash Mode
      Set to: Rear Sync. · Function Lower6
      Only matters when the flash is popped up. Rear Sync fires the flash at the end of the exposure, which gives a nice look at night with moving lights. For normal daytime fill-light, Fill-flash is simpler.

    Viewing, deleting and sending to your phone

    1. ViewPress ▶ (9). Turn the wheel or press ◀ ▶ to go through your photos. Press ● to play a video.
    2. Zoom in to check focusPress AEL / 🔍 (5), then turn the wheel to zoom in or out.
    3. DeletePress C2 / 🗑 (10), choose Delete with ▲, and press ●.
    4. Send to your phoneOn your phone, install Sony's Imaging Edge Mobile app (do this on hotel Wi-Fi before you need it). On the camera, press ▶ and then Fn (Send to Smartphone). The camera shows a Wi-Fi network name and password. Join that network from your phone's Wi-Fi settings, then open the app. JPEG photos transfer; RAW files don't.
    Back to shooting: just half-press the shutter button.

    Battery, charging & memory cards

    Battery lifeAbout 360 shots using the screen, ~310 using the viewfinder. Wi-Fi, video and cold weather drain it faster. Carry a spare (Sony NP-FW50) if you have one.
    ChargingThe battery charges inside the camera. Turn the camera off and plug a micro-USB cable into the port behind the MULTI flap (21). Any phone USB charger or power bank works. In Europe you only need a plug adapter for the USB charger.
    Charge lampOrange = charging. Off = done (a full charge from empty takes about 5 hours, so charge overnight). Flashing = too hot or too cold to charge, so move somewhere room temperature.
    Memory cardSD card, behind the battery door on the bottom. Push it in to release it. Never open the door while the access lamp is lit.
    Changing lensesTurn the camera off first. Hold the camera facing down, and change lenses out of the wind and dust.
    WeatherThe camera is not waterproof. In rain, keep it under your jacket or in a bag. Wipe the lens with a soft cloth, not your shirt.
    Backup habit: every few days, send your favourite shots to your phone. If the camera is lost or stolen, you'll still have them.

    Help! Something's wrong

    The back screen keeps going black
    Something near the viewfinder (your hand, the strap, your body) is triggering the eye sensor. Move it away. If it keeps happening: MENU → custom settings (⚙) → FINDER/MONITOR → set to Monitor.
    Coloured fuzzy outlines on the screen
    That's focus peaking. It shows what's sharp and doesn't appear in your photos. Press C1 (14) to turn it down or off.
    My photos are blurry
    Half-press and wait for the beep and green box before pressing all the way. Hold still and tuck your elbows in. In dim light, use iAuto or choose a smaller f-number in A mode. If the camera focused on the wrong thing, press ● (Focus Area) and choose Wide.
    The flash won't fire
    Press the ⚡ button (3) to pop it up. It never pops up by itself.
    Photos are too bright or too dark
    In iAuto the camera decides. In A mode, use MENU → camera tab → Exposure Comp. and check that Metering (▼) is set to Multi.
    Colours look strange (too blue or too orange)
    Fn → White Balance → AWB (auto).
    Pressing the shutter does nothing
    The card may be full or missing (check the screen for a message), or the camera hasn't been able to focus. Point at something with more contrast and half-press again.
    The viewfinder looks blurry
    Turn the diopter dial (2) beside it.
    “What does this setting mean?”
    Highlight any item in MENU or in the Fn screen and press C2. The camera shows a built-in explanation.
    I changed something and can't undo it
    Turn the mode dial to iAuto and keep shooting. Then message me and we'll sort it out together.
