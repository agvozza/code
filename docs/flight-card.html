The constraint

read first

Aircraft stays over the shoulder — 14 CFR 107.145

No operating over a moving vehicle, so there is no centre pass and no crossing the roadway except through a confirmed-clear gap.

The camera does not have to be above what it photographs. Rotate the drone perpendicular to the roadway, tilt the gimbal across, and translate sideways along the shoulder. Tilt replaces translation.

Nadir only works above 1.8 × your shoulder separation

Shoulder lines cannot be respaced — their separation is the road width. So altitude sets side overlap. At 80 ft separation and 120 ft AGL, two nadir passes share only 53%, and the middle of the road gets the thinnest geometry on the job.

At or above the floor: nadir passes from both shoulders, gimbal −90°. Below it: cross-aimed obliques. The inversion is the point — on a normal grid job nadir is the backbone and obliques supplement it; here it is the other way round.

The GSD cost of staying legal is about 12% — 0.73 cm cross-aimed at 80 ft against 0.65 cm nadir. And shot spacing gets wider, not tighter: yawing the nose across the roadway puts the sensor's long axis along your flight path, so you advance 13 ft between shots instead of the 8 ft a nadir pass needs. GSD is quoted across-look / along-look — an oblique frame is foreshortened in the direction it looks.

Offset is the lateral distance from the shoulder you are flying to the strip you want in frame — shoulder to centerline on a 2-lane is about 40 ft. The nadir floor assumes the opposite shoulder sits at twice that offset. At 90% forward overlap you advance one tenth of a frame per shot: judge it by watching a feature travel a tenth of the way down your screen.

Site notes

from your image
Active traffic. Stop, wait, shoot, advance — never coast while waiting, or you open a gap exactly as long as your patience.
Parked cars in the salvage lot are fine. Stationary objects reconstruct correctly. Only motion between frames corrupts. Don't wait on them.
Fuel canopy, lower left. Tall obstruction and a hard shadow. Note its height before descending.
Treeline moves in wind — same matching problem as traffic, and unavoidable. Fly that edge on a calm day.
Check for overhead utility lines before descending below 80 ft. Hard to see in imagery, hard to see in flight.
