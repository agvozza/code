The shoulder constraint

read first

Altitude floor ≈ 1.8 × your shoulder separation

Flying the shoulders fixes your line separation at the road width. You cannot adjust it — so altitude, not line spacing, is what sets side overlap.

Below 1.8 × the separation the two shoulder lines stop overlapping usefully, and you add a third pass down the centre. That halves the effective separation and restores it. One more parallel pass — not a grid.

80 ft apart → below 142 ft→ add centre pass

With no grid, the obliques carry the whole load

A shoulder-only plan has no crosshatch, so passes 4 and 5 at −55° are the only thing breaking the focal-length/altitude correlation that bends a corridor into a banana. On this plan they are not optional.

The perpendicular crossings and the altitude profiles are the rest of that defence.

−55° is the one that matters. It is 35° off nadir, where two independent studies overlap — MDPI found 20–35° optimal, AeroCartwright 30–45°. A shallower tilt (−75° to −85°) is effectively still a nadir image and will not break the focal-length/height correlation that causes doming.

Site notes

from your image
Active traffic. Stop, wait, shoot, advance — never coast while waiting, or you open a gap exactly as long as your patience.
Parked cars in the salvage lot are fine. Stationary objects reconstruct correctly. Only motion between frames corrupts. Don't wait on them.
Fuel canopy, lower left. Tall obstruction and a hard shadow. Note its height before descending.
Treeline moves in wind — same matching problem as traffic, and unavoidable. Fly that edge on a calm day.
Check for overhead utility lines before descending below 80 ft. Hard to see in imagery, hard to see in flight.
